How Namecheap Stellar billing works

The official pricing page describes billing as "USD per month equivalent; billing term and prepayment depend on plan." In practice, this means the figure shown is a monthly equivalent, not necessarily the amount charged each month. How much is paid up front, and for how long, depends on the plan and the billing term selected at checkout.

The listed term for Namecheap Stellar is 12 months. When comparing it with other line items in a budget, treat the monthly equivalent as a normalized figure and confirm the actual amount due at checkout, since prepayment depends on plan.

Judging total cost: intro price, renewal price and term together

The renewal price for Namecheap Stellar is higher than the intro price. That makes the first term a poor stand-in for the long-run cost of keeping a site online. A realistic cost review looks at three numbers together: the intro price, the renewal price and the length of the term each applies to.

Before approving the plan, read both the intro price and the renewal price in the price table, along with the 12 months term. If the hosting is expected to run beyond the first term, the renewal price is the figure that will drive ongoing spend, so it belongs in any multi-term forecast.

It also helps to record the renewal date as soon as the account is active. Knowing when the higher renewal price takes effect gives time to re-evaluate the plan, change tiers or adjust the budget before the next charge.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Record the billing details

    Right after signing up, note the amount actually charged, the billing term and the renewal date. Because the renewal price is higher than the intro price, add the renewal price from the price table to the budget now. A calendar reminder ahead of the renewal date leaves time to review the plan.

  2. STEP 2Build the site before moving traffic

    Upload or build the site on the new hosting account first, and check that pages, forms and media work as expected. Keep the domain pointed at its current location until the new site is ready. This avoids visitors landing on an empty or half-built site.

  3. STEP 3Connect the domain

    Once the site is ready, point the domain at the new hosting by updating the nameservers or DNS records as instructed by the host. To confirm which nameservers the domain uses now, run this from a local terminal, replacing example.com with the real domain: dig +short NS example.com

  4. STEP 4Enable and verify HTTPS

    After the domain points at the new hosting, set up an SSL certificate for it. The official data does not confirm whether an SSL certificate is included, so check this before signing up. To confirm HTTPS responds and see any redirects, run this locally, replacing example.com with the real domain: curl -I https://example.com

  5. STEP 5Confirm how backups and restores work

    Find out whether backups are made, how often, and how a restore is requested or performed. The official data does not confirm backup coverage, so keep an independent copy of site files and databases until this is verified.
