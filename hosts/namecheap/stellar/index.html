What the official data confirms about Namecheap Stellar

The plan is listed under Shared Hosting, which means Namecheap manages the server and the account holder works with sites, domains and settings rather than with the operating system. The resources on the official pricing page are 20 GB SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth, and the feature list consists of 3 websites. No CPU or RAM figures are given for this plan, so a capacity comparison on those two dimensions is not possible from the data.

Billing is described as a USD per month equivalent, with the note that the billing term and prepayment depend on plan. The term is 12 months. The renewal price is higher than the intro price, so the monthly equivalent shown for the first term does not carry forward unchanged. The price table on this page holds the verified intro price, renewal price and term.

Judging total cost from intro price, renewal price and term

A monthly equivalent is a way of presenting a price, not necessarily the amount charged each month. Because the billing term and prepayment depend on plan, the practical question for a cost review is how much is paid at checkout for the 12 months term and how much is due when that term renews. Reading the two prices and the term together in the table gives the first-term cost and the ongoing cost as separate lines.

A straightforward model has two rows. The first row is the intro monthly equivalent applied across the 12 months term. The second row is the renewal monthly equivalent applied across the same term, repeated for each year the site is expected to stay on the plan. Since renewal is higher than intro, the second row is the one that dominates a multi-year budget, and it is the figure to use when comparing against other hosting lines in a cost sheet.

Treat the intro price as the cost of the first year only. If a product roadmap expects the site to run for several years, the renewal price multiplied by the number of renewal terms is the realistic ongoing figure, with the intro saving counted once.

Who this plan suits

The feature list points to a small footprint: 3 websites, 20 GB SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth. That fits a product team that needs to host a marketing site, a documentation site and perhaps a landing page under one account without managing a server. Teams that need more than 3 websites, or that expect to exceed 20 GB SSD of files and databases, would need to look at the larger Namecheap tiers or at other providers.

A product manager who wants a predictable annual line item will find the 12 months term easy to place in a budget, provided the renewal price is used for every year after the first. Anyone who expects to add sites quickly should note that the site limit is fixed at 3 websites on this tier.

What to check before signing up

How Namecheap Stellar compares with the alternatives

Within the Namecheap range, Stellar Plus and Stellar Business share the same 12 months term, the same billing description and the same pattern of a renewal price higher than the intro price. Stellar Plus lists Unlimited websites, Unlimited SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth. Stellar Business lists Unlimited websites, 50 GB SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth. Stellar is the tier with 3 websites and 20 GB SSD, so the choice among the three comes down to how many sites and how much storage the roadmap needs, with the price table showing what each tier costs in the first term and at renewal.

The hosting.com plans use a different billing structure. hosting.com Starter is billed as USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, with a 48 months term, 5 websites, 3 staging websites and 15 GB web space. hosting.com Pro is billed the same way on a 48 months term with Up to 10 websites, 20 staging websites and 50 GB web space. The data for the hosting.com plans does not state how renewal compares with the intro price and does not list bandwidth, so those points need to be confirmed with hosting.com.

For a cost review, the key difference is cash flow and commitment length. A 48 months term paid upfront concentrates spending at the start and fixes the per-month rate for four years, while the 12 months term on Namecheap Stellar spreads spending across shorter cycles but moves to the higher renewal price after the first year. A team that values flexibility and a smaller initial payment would lean toward the 12 months term; a team that prefers to lock a rate for a longer planning horizon and can pay upfront would lean toward the 48 months term. The staging websites on the hosting.com plans are also a feature the Namecheap data does not list, which matters for teams that test releases before publishing.
