Reading intro price, renewal price and term as one number

For a cost review, the three figures that matter are the intro price, the renewal price and the term, and they only make sense together. The term here is 12 months, so the intro price in the table applies to that first period. Because the renewal price is listed as higher, the price that applies after the first 12 months is not the same figure; read the renewal price and term in the table before modeling anything beyond year one.

A practical way to frame this for a budget line: the first period is priced at the intro rate, and every following period is priced at the renewal rate. A multi-year estimate that repeats the intro price overstates the discount and understates the recurring spend. The table supplies both figures; the text here deliberately leaves them out so that the verified numbers are the only ones on the page.

The billing field says USD per month equivalent, which means the displayed figure is a per-month equivalent rather than a statement that the plan is charged month by month. The same field says the billing term and prepayment depend on plan, so the listing does not pin down how much is prepaid up front for the 12 months term. Confirm the actual invoice amount and payment schedule with Namecheap before approving the spend, since a prepaid period changes cash flow timing even when the per-month equivalent stays the same.

  2. STEP 2Connect the domain to the hosting account

    Use the nameserver or DNS details Namecheap supplies for the account to point the domain at the new hosting. If the site is moving from another host, finish building and testing it on the new account before changing DNS so visitors are not sent to an unfinished site. To confirm which nameservers the domain currently uses, replace example.com with the real domain and run: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable HTTPS once DNS resolves

    Issue or activate an SSL certificate only after the domain resolves to the new hosting, because issuance typically depends on the domain being reachable there. The plan listing does not confirm whether a certificate is included, so check with Namecheap before signing up and factor any cost into the total. After activation, replace example.com with the real domain and confirm the secure response and any redirects with: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Confirm how backups and restores work

    The listing does not mention backups for this plan, so find out from Namecheap whether backups are included, how often they run and how a restore is requested before relying on them. If they are not included, decide whether a separate backup method is needed and add its cost to the forecast. Document the restore process so it is not being learned for the first time during an incident.
