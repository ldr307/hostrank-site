Who Namecheap Stellar Business suits

Stellar Business fits a small business that wants shared hosting where the provider manages the server, so the owner can focus on the website rather than server administration. Its Unlimited websites feature can help a business that plans more than one site, such as a main company site plus a separate site for a product line, a landing page or a local branch.

The plan lists 50 GB SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth. For a typical company website with pages, images and a contact form, these are the resources to weigh against your own content. The official data for this plan does not list CPU or RAM figures, so if your site depends on heavier workloads, confirm those details with Namecheap before signing up.

What to check before signing up

Billing is shown as USD per month equivalent, and the billing term and prepayment depend on the plan. In practice, the monthly figure is an equivalent rate, and you may be asked to pay for the full term up front. The listed term is 12 months.

The renewal price for Stellar Business is higher than the intro price. Before you commit, read both the intro price and the renewal price, along with the term, in the price table on this page so the cost after the first term is not a surprise.

Several things a business site usually needs are not listed in the plan's features: an SSL certificate, business email and automatic backups. The official data does not confirm whether these are included, so check each one with Namecheap before signing up.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Build and review the site before going live

    Set up your company website in the hosting account first, using the account's temporary or preview options if available. Check your pages, contact details and contact form. Do not point your domain at the new hosting until the site is ready for customers to see.

  2. STEP 2Connect your business domain

    Once the site is ready, point your domain at the hosting by updating the nameservers or DNS records at your domain registrar, following the values Namecheap provides for your account. To see which nameservers your domain uses now, run this command from your own computer, replacing example.com with your domain: dig +short NS example.com. Run it again after the change to confirm the new nameservers appear.

  3. STEP 3Turn on HTTPS

    After the domain points at the new hosting, enable an SSL certificate so visitors see a secure connection. Check with Namecheap how certificates are provided on this plan, since this is not confirmed in the listed features. To confirm HTTPS responds and see any redirects, run this command, replacing example.com with your domain: curl -I https://example.com.

  4. STEP 4Set up business email

    Decide where your company email will live, such as addresses for sales and support. Confirm whether this plan includes email for your domain or whether a separate email service is needed. Send and receive a test message from each address before printing it on cards or invoices.

  5. STEP 5Confirm backups and note billing details

    Find out how backups and restores work for your account, and keep your own copy of the site files and database as well. Write down the renewal date for your term of 12 months and check the renewal price in the price table, since it is higher than the intro price. Set a reminder ahead of the renewal date so you can review the plan in time.
