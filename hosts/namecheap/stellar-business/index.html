Who this plan suits

Namecheap Stellar Business fits a business that wants to run its main company website and possibly additional sites, such as a separate landing page for a campaign or a second brand, under one hosting account. The plan lists Unlimited websites, so the number of sites is not the limiting factor; the 50 GB SSD storage is the resource to measure against your needs.

Because the provider manages the server, the owner does not need to maintain an operating system, apply server patches or configure a web server. That makes it a reasonable fit for an owner who wants to focus on the site content and the business rather than on infrastructure.

A company that expects very large media libraries, long archives of product photos or heavy downloadable files should compare the 50 GB SSD figure against its actual file sizes before signing up. Bandwidth is listed as Unmetered, so traffic volume is less of a concern than storage.

Checking the domain before and after moving

If the company domain is already registered, the first practical check is which nameservers it currently uses. Replace example.com with your own domain and run this from your own computer: dig +short NS example.com

Once hosting is set up and the domain points to the new account, confirm that the site answers over HTTPS and whether it redirects from HTTP. Again replace example.com with your domain: curl -I https://example.com

If the HTTPS response shows an error, the most common causes for a newly connected business site are that DNS has not yet updated or that the certificate has not been issued on the hosting account. The official data does not state how long DNS changes take at this provider, so recheck later rather than assuming a problem.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Connect the company domain

    After signing up, find the nameservers or DNS records the hosting account expects, using the provider's own documentation. If the domain is registered elsewhere, update its nameservers or point its records at the new hosting account. Keep the existing site live until the new site is ready, and only move the domain once the content is in place.

  2. STEP 2Enable HTTPS once the domain points at the hosting

    An SSL certificate is not confirmed in the official data, so check with Namecheap how one is obtained for this plan. Issue or activate it only after the domain resolves to the new account, because validation generally depends on the domain pointing at the right place. Then load the site over HTTPS and confirm there are no browser warnings.

Ask the provider how backups are taken and how a restore is requested. Keep an independent copy of the site files and database somewhere outside the hosting account as well.
