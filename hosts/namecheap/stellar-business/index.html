Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Build and review the site first

    Upload or build the company website in the new hosting account before changing anything about the domain. Review the main pages, contact forms and images so the site is ready for visitors. Keep any existing site live until the new one is complete.

  2. STEP 2Connect the business domain

    Point the domain to the new hosting by updating its nameservers or DNS records at the domain registrar, using the values Namecheap provides for the account. To see which nameservers the domain currently uses, run this command and replace example.com with the business domain: dig +short NS example.com. Changes can take some time to spread across the internet.

  3. STEP 3Turn on HTTPS

    Once the domain points at the new hosting, enable an SSL certificate using whatever option Namecheap offers for the account, as discussed above. Then confirm that the site loads securely and redirects correctly by running this command with your own domain in place of example.com: curl -I https://example.com. Look for a successful response over HTTPS.

  4. STEP 4Set up email and confirm backups

    Set up email on the company domain using the service you confirmed with Namecheap, and send test messages in both directions. Find out how backups and restores work for the account, and keep your own copy of the site files and database as an extra safeguard. Test a restore if the account allows it.

  5. STEP 5Note the renewal details

    Record the renewal date at the end of the term of 12 months. Since the renewal price is higher than the intro price, check the renewal price in the price table and plan the budget for it ahead of time.
