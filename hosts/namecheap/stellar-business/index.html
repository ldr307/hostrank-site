What the official data lists for Namecheap Stellar Business

The pricing page lists three resource-related details for this plan: 50 GB SSD storage, Unmetered bandwidth and a single feature line, Unlimited websites. There is no listed CPU or RAM figure, which is common for Shared Hosting, where the provider manages the server and does not publish per-account processor or memory allocations.

The billing field reads USD per month equivalent; billing term and prepayment depend on plan, and the term is 12 months. In plain terms, the monthly figure shown in the price table is an equivalent, and the amount actually charged up front depends on the plan and term selected. Before paying, look at the checkout summary to see how much is collected for the 12 months term rather than assuming a month-by-month charge.

The renewal price is higher than the introductory price. The price table on this page shows both figures, so read the renewal price and term in the table and treat the renewal figure as the ongoing cost of running the company site after the first term ends.

Who this plan suits

Namecheap Stellar Business fits a business owner who expects to run more than one site on a single account. The Unlimited websites feature means a main company site, a separate landing page for a campaign, a second brand or a staging copy can all sit under the same plan without buying another hosting account. If the business only ever needs a single site, the Unlimited websites feature will go unused, and the comparison section below covers plans with a smaller site allowance.

The 50 GB SSD storage figure is the main capacity limit to keep in mind. A brochure-style company site with service pages, product photos and downloadable documents usually takes up far less than that, while a large archive of high-resolution images or video files hosted directly on the server will use it much faster. Bandwidth is listed as Unmetered, so the official data gives no monthly transfer figure to watch.

The 12 months term and higher renewal make this plan suit a business that plans to keep the site online for at least a year and can budget for the renewal figure, rather than a short-lived project that will be taken down within a few months.

What to check before signing up

Because the feature list for this plan contains only Unlimited websites, several items a company website normally depends on are not confirmed by the official data, and each one should be verified with Namecheap before paying. The domain itself is the first: the data does not say whether a domain registration is included, so a business that already owns its domain should expect to keep it at the current registrar and point it at the hosting account, while a new business should ask whether registration is part of the plan or a separate purchase.

HTTPS is the second item. The official data does not confirm an SSL certificate for this plan, and a company site without one shows a browser warning that puts customers off contact forms and checkout pages. Ask Namecheap whether a certificate is included and how it is enabled before relying on it.

Business email is the third item. The data does not confirm mailboxes, webmail or mail forwarding, so a business that wants addresses on its own domain should confirm what the plan provides or plan to host email with another service and point the domain's mail records there. The fourth item is backups: the official data does not confirm automatic backups or a restore tool, so find out what Namecheap offers and keep an independent copy of the site regardless. The same check applies to the control panel, migration help and support channels, none of which appear in the feature list.

Finally, read the billing details carefully. The 12 months term, the prepayment collected at checkout and the higher renewal price all affect what the site costs over time, and the price table on this page carries the verified figures.

Common launch problems for a company site

If the domain still shows the old host or a parking page after you change the nameservers, the change usually has not spread to every DNS resolver yet. Check that the nameservers were saved correctly at the registrar and give the change time before troubleshooting the hosting account itself. Do not switch the domain over until the new site is finished, because customers will see whatever is on the new account the moment the change takes effect.

If the browser shows a not secure warning after the move, either the certificate has not been enabled for the domain yet or the site still serves pages over plain http. Confirm the certificate is active for both the bare domain and the www version, then make sure every page redirects to the https address.

If company email stops arriving after the nameserver change, the MX records that direct mail were probably not copied to the new DNS zone. Record the existing mail records before changing nameservers and add them again on the new DNS so mail continues to reach the right service.

    After the account is active, find the nameservers or DNS records Namecheap assigns to the plan and enter them at the registrar that holds your domain. If the domain already handles company email elsewhere, write down the existing MX records before changing anything so mail keeps flowing. To see which nameservers the domain currently uses, run this on your own computer and replace example.com with your domain: dig +short NS example.com

  2. STEP 2Enable HTTPS once the domain resolves

    Wait until the domain loads the new hosting account rather than the old location, then enable a certificate using the method Namecheap confirmed for your plan when you asked before signing up. Make sure plain http addresses redirect to https so customers never see a browser warning on contact or payment pages. Confirm the result from your computer, replacing example.com with your domain: curl -I https://example.com

  3. STEP 3Set up business email and a backup routine

    Create the mailboxes your company needs through the email option you verified is available, and update the domain's MX records if mail is hosted somewhere other than the website. Then decide how backups will work: use whatever Namecheap provides, and also keep an independent copy of the site files and database on storage you control. Run a test restore once so the process is familiar before a real problem happens.

  4. STEP 4Record the billing details

    Note that the plan runs on a 12 months term and that the official data shows the renewal price is higher than the first-term price. Put the renewal date in your business calendar and read the renewal price and term in the price table so the renewal does not surprise your bookkeeping.
