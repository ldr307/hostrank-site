What the official data lists for Stellar Plus

The plan belongs to the Shared Hosting category, which means Namecheap manages the server and the agency works at the site and account level. The resources listed on the official pricing page are Unlimited SSD for storage and Unmetered for bandwidth. The feature list contains a single entry: Unlimited websites.

The term is 12 months. Billing is described as a USD per month equivalent, with the billing term and prepayment depending on the plan. In practice that means the per-month figure in the table is a way of expressing the cost, not necessarily the amount charged each month; the table shows what the term and prepayment look like for this plan.

The data does not list CPU or RAM for this plan, and beyond Unlimited websites it does not mention backups, an SSL certificate, a control panel, email accounts, staging environments or separate client logins. An agency that depends on any of those should confirm them with Namecheap before moving client sites.

Who this plan suits

Stellar Plus fits an agency that wants to consolidate a number of smaller client sites on one Shared Hosting account without counting sites against a cap. Unlimited websites and Unlimited SSD remove the two limits that most often force an agency onto a second account, and Unmetered bandwidth means traffic volume is not a listed constraint either.

Consolidation also concentrates risk. One account carries one login, one renewal date and one shared pool of server resources, so a problem with any of those affects every client site at once. Agencies that bill clients separately for hosting, or that need to hand a client full control of a single site later, should ask Namecheap whether sites can be separated or transferred before committing all of them to one account.

Because the data gives no CPU or RAM figures, this page cannot say how many busy sites the plan handles comfortably. Treat it as a home for sites with modest traffic until the agency has its own usage history on the account.

Billing, term and renewal: what to check before signing up

The table shows the price as a USD per month equivalent. Read the term column alongside it: the plan carries a 12 months term, and the billing description says the billing term and prepayment depend on the plan, so the first charge may cover the whole term rather than a single month. Confirm the exact prepayment amount in the table before entering a card.

The renewal price is higher than the intro price. For an agency this matters twice: once for its own budget and once for any client it resells hosting to, because quotes based on the first-term figure will be wrong from the second term onward. Read the renewal price and term in the table and base client pricing on the renewal figure, not the intro figure.

Note the renewal date at the moment of purchase. With every client site on the same account, a missed renewal would take down all of them, so the date belongs in the agency's shared calendar rather than in one person's inbox.

Keeping backups, renewal dates and access organised across client sites

Backups: since the listed features do not cover backups, plan on the agency keeping its own copies regardless of what Namecheap provides. Schedule a regular export of each client site's files and database using whatever export method the account offers, store the copies outside the hosting account, and label them by client and date. Test a restore on one low-risk site before relying on the routine.

Renewal dates: keep a single register that maps each client domain to the Stellar Plus account, the 12 months term, the renewal date and the renewal price from the table. When a client leaves, update the register before the site is removed so nobody renews hosting for a site that no longer exists.

Access: the data does not say whether individual client logins or team-member roles are available, so assume a single account login until Namecheap confirms otherwise. Store that login in a password manager with access limited to the people who manage hosting, record which domains use Namecheap nameservers versus external DNS, and document the process for adding or removing a site so that handovers between staff do not break anything.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Record the account, term and renewal details

    Right after checkout, write down the account login, the order date, the term shown in the table and the resulting renewal date, plus the renewal price from the table. Put the login in the agency password manager and the renewal date in a shared calendar. Keep a register that lists every client domain added to this account so renewals and removals stay traceable.

  2. STEP 2Add each client site and point its domain

    Create each client site in the hosting account and upload or migrate its files and database before touching DNS. Then update the domain's nameservers or its A and AAAA records to the values Namecheap provides for the account. From a local terminal, confirm which nameservers a domain currently uses, replacing example.com with the client's domain: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable a certificate once the domain resolves

    After DNS points at the new hosting, enable an SSL certificate for each client domain using whatever method Namecheap provides for the account. Only then switch the site's address to HTTPS and add a redirect. Verify the secure response and any redirect from a local terminal, again replacing example.com with the client's domain: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Set up backups and test a restore

    Find out from Namecheap how backups and restores work on this account, then set up the agency's own scheduled exports of files and databases for every client site. Store the copies outside the hosting account, labelled by client and date. Perform a test restore on one low-risk site so the procedure is proven before a client needs it.
