What the official data lists for Namecheap Stellar Plus

The data for Namecheap Stellar Plus is short. The feature list contains one item, Unlimited websites. Storage is listed as Unmetered SSD and bandwidth as Unmetered. The term is 12 months, and billing is described as a USD per month equivalent, where the billing term and prepayment depend on plan. The renewal price is listed as higher than the intro price.

No CPU or RAM figures appear in the data for this plan, so the page cannot say how much processing capacity a single account gets or how many concurrent visitors across all client sites it can absorb. For an agency that plans to put many production sites on one shared account, that is the first thing to look up on the provider's site before deciding.

Who Namecheap Stellar Plus suits

The plan is most relevant to an agency that wants one hosting account for a growing list of client sites and does not want to count add-on domains against a cap each time a new client is onboarded. Unlimited websites is the one feature the data confirms, and it is the feature that matters most in that scenario.

Unmetered SSD and Unmetered bandwidth mean the pricing page does not publish a storage or transfer figure for this plan. Unmetered is not the same as a published number, so the sensible step is to read Namecheap's terms of use for how unmetered resources are defined and what happens if one client site grows unusually large. If staging websites are part of the agency workflow, see the comparison section below, since two of the alternative plans list them and this plan's feature list does not.

How billing, the term and renewal work

Billing is shown as a USD per month equivalent, and the official data states that the billing term and prepayment depend on plan. In practice that means the monthly figure in the table is a way of expressing the price, not necessarily a month-to-month charge, and the amount collected at checkout follows the term you select. The term listed for this plan is 12 months.

The renewal price is higher than the intro price. For an agency that re-bills hosting to clients, the renewal price is the figure to use for budgeting beyond the first term, so read the renewal price and the term in the table before quoting a client a monthly hosting fee. Putting the renewal date in the agency's billing calendar at signup avoids a surprise when the first term ends.

Keeping backups, access and renewal dates organised across client sites

Backups are the biggest gap. The feature list does not mention backups, so the official data does not confirm whether backups are included, how often they are taken, or how a restore is run. Check this with Namecheap before signing up, and plan to keep off-host copies of every client site regardless, because a client asking for a rollback is the most common support request an agency receives after a change goes wrong.

Access is the second gap. The data does not confirm a control panel, SSH access, or whether separate logins can be created per client site, so an agency should confirm how access is granted before promising a client their own login. Until that is confirmed, treat the account as one agency-owned login, store its credentials in the agency password manager, and keep a simple register that maps each client domain to the folder it lives in and the person responsible for it.

Renewal dates are easier to manage because there is only one hosting term for the whole account. Record the renewal date for this plan alongside each client's domain expiry date, since domain renewals are handled separately from hosting and often fall on different dates. When a client leaves, that register tells you which folders and domains to remove so the account stays tidy.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Record the account details and the renewal date

    As soon as the order completes, store the account login in the agency password manager and note the term shown in the table together with the date it ends. Because the renewal price is higher than the first-term price, read the renewal price in the table and record it next to the renewal date so client quotes already reflect it. Add the renewal date to the shared billing calendar.

  2. STEP 2Add the first client site and connect its domain

    Add the client's domain to the hosting account and upload or build the site there first, so the new copy is ready before any traffic moves. Then update the domain's nameservers or DNS records as Namecheap's own documentation describes for this plan. To confirm which nameservers the domain is using now, run this from a local machine and replace example.com with the client's domain: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable an SSL certificate once the domain resolves

    The official data does not confirm that an SSL certificate is included with this plan, so check this with Namecheap before signing up. After the domain points at the new hosting, enable or install the certificate for the client domain through whatever method the account provides. To confirm HTTPS answers and see any redirects, run this locally and replace example.com with the client's domain: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Set up the backup routine before the site goes live

    Find out in the hosting account how backups are taken and how a restore is run, and perform one test restore on a non-client site so the procedure is documented before it is needed. Schedule off-host copies of each client site and record where they are stored in the client register. Repeat the domain, certificate and backup steps for every additional client site added to the account.
