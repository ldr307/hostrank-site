What the official data lists for Namecheap Stellar Plus

The plan sits in the Shared Hosting category, which means Namecheap manages the server and the agency works at the site and account level rather than at the operating system level. The single listed feature is Unlimited websites. Storage is listed as Unlimited SSD and bandwidth as Unmetered.

The data does not list CPU or RAM figures for this plan, and it does not mention a control panel, staging sites, backups, SSL certificates or email. None of those should be assumed. The sections below note where each one matters for an agency and what to confirm with Namecheap before paying.

Who Namecheap Stellar Plus suits

An agency that adds client sites regularly and does not want to count sites against a cap is the natural fit for the Unlimited websites feature. Compared with Namecheap Stellar, which lists 3 websites, Stellar Plus removes the site count as the trigger for an upgrade.

Unlimited SSD storage and Unmetered bandwidth follow the same pattern: the official data states no cap in either case. Read those labels as written and check the provider's terms for how they are applied in day-to-day use, because the data itself says nothing beyond the labels.

The plan suits less well an agency that needs listed staging environments per client, or that needs documented CPU and RAM allocations to size a workload, since neither is listed for this plan.

How billing, the term and renewal work

The billing field reads USD per month equivalent; billing term and prepayment depend on plan. In plain terms, the price is expressed as a monthly figure, but how much is paid up front and over what period depends on the plan and the option selected at checkout. The term listed for Stellar Plus is 12 months.

The renewal price is higher than the intro price. For an agency this matters twice: once for the hosting budget, and once for how the cost is passed on to clients. Read the renewal price and the term in the price table before signing up, and build the renewal figure rather than the first-term figure into client quotes.

Put the renewal date into whatever system the agency already uses to track domain and certificate expiry, alongside the plan name and the list of client sites that live on it. A single shared hosting account carrying many client sites becomes a single point of failure if its renewal lapses unnoticed.

Backups, access and keeping many client sites organized

Backups: the official data for Stellar Plus does not mention backups or restores. Do not assume that provider-side backups exist, how often they run, or whether a single client site can be restored without touching the others. Ask Namecheap before signing up, and plan an agency-side routine regardless, exporting each client site separately and storing the copies outside the hosting account.

Access: the data does not describe how logins are separated between sites or whether a client can be given credentials limited to their own site. An agency that needs to hand a client restricted access should confirm whether that is possible. In the meantime, keep the owner login in a password manager and maintain a shared inventory that records which domains, databases and contacts belong to which client.

Domains and DNS: with many client domains pointing at one account, keep a record of where each domain is registered and which nameservers it uses. That record is what makes a migration or a renewal problem manageable later, because the agency can see at a glance which domains would be affected.

SSL and staging: neither SSL certificates nor staging sites appear in the feature list for this plan. Both hosting.com alternatives list staging websites, which may matter to an agency that previews changes before pushing them live. Confirm with Namecheap whether certificates and staging are included and how they are handled per site.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Record the account and build the client inventory

    Store the account login in the agency password manager before adding any client site. Create an inventory entry for each client that records the domain, the registrar, the database name and the client contact, so that every later task starts from a known list. Keep this inventory outside the hosting account itself.

  2. STEP 2Point each client domain at the account

    Set up the first client site in the account, then update that client's nameservers or DNS records at the registrar to point at the new hosting. Do this one site at a time, and only after the site is ready to serve traffic. Check which nameservers a domain currently uses from a local machine, replacing example.com with the client's domain: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable an SSL certificate once DNS resolves

    After the domain points at the account, enable a certificate for it using whatever method the account provides. Confirm that HTTPS responds and that any redirect from HTTP lands where expected, replacing example.com with the client's domain: curl -I https://example.com Repeat the check for each client domain added later.

  4. STEP 4Set up backups and a restore routine

    Find out how backups and restores work for the account, including whether one client site can be restored on its own. Independently of that, schedule an agency-side export of each client site's files and database to storage outside the hosting account, and test a restore of at least one site so the procedure is known before it is needed.

  5. STEP 5Note the renewal date and renewal price

    The plan has a 12 months term and the renewal price is higher than the intro price. Record the renewal date and the renewal price from the price table in the same tracking system used for domain and certificate expiry, together with the list of client sites on the account. Confirm at checkout which prepayment option applies, since the billing wording leaves this to the plan chosen.
