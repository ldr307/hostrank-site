Who Namecheap Stellar Plus suits

The plan's feature list contains one item: Unlimited websites. For an agency that hosts several small client sites under one account, that is the central point. You can add client projects without the plan's website allowance being the limit you plan around.

Storage is listed as Unlimited SSD and bandwidth as Unmetered. Those are the resources shown on the official pricing page, and they are the only resource details available for this plan. CPU and RAM figures are not part of the official data, so if your client sites run heavier workloads, confirm those resources with Namecheap before you consolidate them here.

Because this is Shared Hosting, the provider manages the server. That suits agencies that want to focus on building and maintaining client sites rather than administering servers.

Keeping many client sites organized on one account

When one account holds many client sites, the risk shifts from resources to organization. Keep a single register that lists each client domain, where its DNS is managed, who holds registrar access, and the date the hosting term ends. A shared, access-controlled record works better than details spread across inboxes.

Treat the hosting renewal as one date that affects every client at once. Put it in the agency calendar with an early reminder, and record the renewal price from the table so it can be checked against what clients are billed.

Backups deserve the same discipline. Decide which sites are backed up, how often, and where copies are stored outside the hosting account, and record who is allowed to run a restore. The setup guide below covers how to confirm what the plan itself provides.

Stellar Plus compared with Stellar and Stellar Business

Namecheap lists two alternatives in the same category. All three are Shared Hosting plans with a 12 months term, Unmetered bandwidth, and a renewal price that is higher than the intro price.

Namecheap Stellar lists 3 websites and 20 GB SSD storage. It fits an agency or freelancer with only a few client sites who does not need room for more projects on one account.

Both remove the website count as a limit, so agencies weighing them should compare the storage wording and the prices in the table, and ask Namecheap about any other differences that matter to their clients.

No single option fits every agency. The right choice depends on how many client sites you plan to host and on what the price table shows for each plan after renewal.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Build a client and access register

    Before adding any site, list every client domain, its registrar, who holds the login, and where its DNS is managed. Record the start date of the 12 months term and the renewal price from the price table, since the renewal price is higher than the intro price. This single record keeps renewal dates and access in one place for the whole team.

  2. STEP 2Prepare each site before moving traffic

    Set up and test each client site in the hosting account before changing any DNS records. To see where a client domain points today, run this, replacing example.com with the client domain: dig +short NS example.com. Only update nameservers or DNS records once the new site is ready.

  3. STEP 3Enable an SSL certificate after DNS points to the host

    Once a domain points at the new hosting, enable a certificate for it. The official data does not confirm whether an SSL certificate is included, so check this with Namecheap before signing up. To confirm HTTPS works and see any redirects, run this, replacing example.com with the client domain: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Confirm how backups and restores work

    The official data does not confirm a backup feature, so ask Namecheap what is backed up, how often, and how a restore is requested. Whatever the answer, keep your own copies of each client site outside the hosting account. Record in the register who may trigger a restore for each client.

  5. STEP 5Schedule the renewal review

    Add the end of the term to the agency calendar with an early reminder, because every client site depends on it. Before that date, compare the renewal price in the price table with what clients are billed. Review the client register at the same time so logins and contacts stay current.
