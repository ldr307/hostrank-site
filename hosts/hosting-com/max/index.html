What the official data lists for hosting.com Max

The feature list for hosting.com Max is short and specific: Up to 50 websites and 200 staging websites. Storage is listed as 100 GB web space. The plan sits in the Shared Hosting category, which means hosting.com runs and maintains the server and the store owner works inside a hosting account rather than administering a machine.

Billing is described as USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront. In plain terms, the price is expressed as a monthly figure, but the full 48 months are charged in a single payment when the order is placed. The monthly figure is a way of expressing the cost, not a monthly charge to a card. The exact amounts are in the price table on this page.

Who hosting.com Max suits

This plan fits a store owner who runs more than one storefront or wants to keep the store separate from brand sites, campaign landing pages and a blog, all under one hosting account. The Up to 50 websites allowance leaves room for that, and 100 GB web space is the space available for product images, theme files and uploads across those sites.

The 200 staging websites are the feature that matters most for a store. A staging website is a working copy of a site used to try changes before they go live. For a shop, that means testing a theme update, a new payment plugin or a checkout redesign on a copy first, so a broken step never reaches a real customer. A store owner who edits the live checkout directly is taking an avoidable risk, and this plan gives a lot of room to avoid it.

It is less suited to someone who wants to pay as they go. The whole 4-year term is paid upfront, so the commitment is made on day one. An owner with a single small shop and no plans for additional sites may find hosting.com Pro or TMDHosting Starter, described below, a closer match.

What to check before signing up

Billing comes first. The term is 48 months, paid upfront, which means the outlay happens once and the hosting is then in place for the full period. The official data does not list a renewal price for hosting.com Max, so confirm with hosting.com what the plan costs when the term ends and how that renewal is charged before paying. Also ask about the refund policy, since none is listed for this plan.

Second, check what is included beyond the three listed items. The feature list covers websites, staging websites and web space only; it does not confirm an SSL certificate, backups, bandwidth, a control panel, email accounts or a free domain. For a store, the SSL certificate and backups are not optional extras, so get written answers from hosting.com on both before signing up: whether a certificate is included and renews automatically, and whether backups are taken, how often, and how a restore is requested.

Third, think about the payment flow. If card details are collected on the store's own pages rather than on a payment provider's hosted page, the store takes on compliance obligations that a shared plan may or may not support. Nothing in the data speaks to this, so ask hosting.com directly if that is the planned setup.

Protecting the checkout page: HTTPS and backups on a shared plan

Every page of a store should load over HTTPS, not just the checkout. Browsers flag pages that mix secure and insecure content, and a customer who sees a warning while entering an address will often abandon the cart. Once the domain points at hosting.com and the certificate is active, force all plain HTTP requests to redirect to HTTPS and update the store's base URL in its settings so internal links and images use the secure address.

A quick way to confirm the certificate and the redirect from any computer is to request the response headers, replacing example.com with the store's domain: curl -I https://example.com

Backups protect the order history and the product catalog, which are the two things a store cannot easily rebuild. Whatever hosting.com provides, keep an independent copy as well: an export of the store database and a copy of the uploads folder, stored somewhere outside the hosting account. Then use one of the staging websites to practice a restore, so the first time a restore is attempted is not during an outage with orders coming in.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Point the store's domain at hosting.com

    After signup, hosting.com will tell you which nameservers or DNS records to use; apply them at the registrar where the domain is managed. If the store already exists elsewhere, build and test it on the new account first and change DNS only when the copy is ready, so customers never land on an empty site. To see which nameservers the domain uses now, run this from any computer, replacing example.com with your domain: dig +short NS example.com

  2. STEP 2Enable HTTPS and force the redirect

    Once the domain resolves to hosting.com, activate the SSL certificate: turn on the one hosting.com provides if your written confirmation says one is included, or install a certificate you obtain yourself. Set the store to redirect all HTTP requests to HTTPS and update the site address in the store settings. Open the checkout page in a browser and confirm the padlock appears with no mixed-content warning.

  3. STEP 3Set up backups and rehearse a restore

    Confirm with hosting.com what is backed up, how often, how long copies are kept and how a restore is requested. Separately, export the store database and download the uploads folder on a regular schedule and keep those copies outside the hosting account. Restore one of them onto a staging website to make sure the process works before it is needed.

  4. STEP 4Record the billing details

    The plan is paid upfront for 48 months, so write down the date the term ends and set a reminder ahead of it. Confirm the renewal price and how renewal is charged with hosting.com and keep that answer with your records. Also keep the payment receipt, since a store's hosting cost is a business expense.
