What the official data lists for hosting.com Pro

The plan sits in the Shared Hosting category, so hosting.com runs and maintains the server and the blogger works inside a hosting account rather than at a command line. The listed features are up to 10 websites and 20 staging websites, and the listed storage is 50 GB web space. Those three items are the only resource details the official pricing page provides for this plan.

The term is 48 months. The billing field reads USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront. In plain words, the price is expressed as a monthly figure, but the full 4-year term is charged in one payment at signup. The exact amount appears in the verified price table on this page.

The official data does not list a renewal price for hosting.com Pro, so there is no way to tell from the data whether the cost after the first 48 months will be higher, lower or the same. Confirm the renewal price with hosting.com before paying.

Who this plan suits

A solo blogger with one WordPress blog does not need ten sites, but the allowance of up to 10 websites leaves room for a second project, a newsletter landing page or a portfolio site later without changing plans. The 20 staging websites are the more relevant feature for a blog: a staging site is generally a separate copy of a site used to try a new theme, test a plugin update or rework a layout before the live blog changes. The official data does not describe how staging works on this plan, so check the details in the hosting.com account or with the provider.

The plan fits someone who is comfortable paying for 48 months in one go and who intends to keep the blog running for years. The upfront payment means the decision is harder to undo than a monthly or yearly plan, so it suits a blogger who has already settled on a niche and a domain rather than someone testing an idea.

It is a weaker fit for a blogger who wants to spread payments out, who expects to outgrow shared hosting soon, or who needs confirmation of specific extras before committing. The next section covers what the data leaves open.

What the data does not confirm for a WordPress blog

Several things a blogger usually looks for are not mentioned in the official data for hosting.com Pro: a WordPress installer, an SSL certificate, backups and restores, email accounts, a control panel, and the amount of CPU, RAM and bandwidth. None of these should be assumed to be included or excluded. Check each one in the hosting.com account or with the provider before signing up, since a blog needs at least HTTPS and a backup routine from day one.

The data also says nothing about uptime, speed, support hours or server locations, and this page does not estimate them. What the data does make clear is the shape of the deal: a long term, paid upfront, with a fixed storage figure and site allowances. Weigh that against how long the blog is likely to run.

How hosting.com Pro compares with the alternatives

hosting.com Starter is the closest alternative. It is also billed in USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, with a 48 months term, but it lists 5 websites, 3 staging websites and 15 GB web space. For a single WordPress blog that will stay a single blog, Starter covers the basics; Pro makes sense when the extra staging websites, the larger web space or the higher site count are likely to be used.

Namecheap Stellar lists 3 websites, 20 GB SSD and Unmetered bandwidth on a 12 months term. Namecheap Stellar Plus lists Unlimited websites, Unlimited SSD and Unmetered bandwidth, and Namecheap Stellar Business lists Unlimited websites, 50 GB SSD and Unmetered bandwidth, both also on a 12 months term. Billing for all three is described as USD per month equivalent, with the billing term and prepayment depending on the plan.

Two differences stand out. First, the Namecheap plans carry a 12 months term against 48 months for the hosting.com plans, so a blogger who wants a shorter commitment has that option there. Second, the Namecheap plans have a renewal price that is higher than their first-term price, while hosting.com Pro has no renewal price in the data at all, so the longer-run cost of each option needs to be worked out before choosing. Note that the Namecheap plans state bandwidth and describe storage as SSD, whereas hosting.com lists web space without a bandwidth figure; these are different descriptions and are not directly comparable.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Install WordPress and build the blog before pointing the domain

    Install WordPress on the new web space using whatever method the hosting.com account offers, then set up the theme, the essential plugins and a first post. Doing this before the domain moves means visitors never land on an empty site. If a staging website is available, this is also the moment to learn how to create one, since the plan lists 20 staging websites.

  2. STEP 2Point the domain at hosting.com

    In the account where the domain is registered, either change the nameservers to the ones hosting.com provides or update the DNS records to the values shown in the hosting.com account. To see which nameservers the domain uses right now, run this from any computer, replacing example.com with the blog's domain: dig +short NS example.com. Wait until the new values show up before moving on.

  3. STEP 3Enable HTTPS once DNS points at the new site

    The official data does not confirm whether an SSL certificate is included, so check in the hosting.com account or with the provider and enable or install a certificate after the domain resolves to hosting.com. Then set the WordPress site address to https and make sure HTTP requests redirect. To confirm the certificate and the redirect are working, run this and replace example.com with the blog's domain: curl -I https://example.com.

  4. STEP 4Find out how backups and restores work

    Backups are not mentioned in the official data for this plan, so look in the hosting.com account for any backup feature and ask the provider how a restore is performed and how far back backups go. If nothing is included, add a WordPress backup plugin that stores copies away from the hosting account. Test a restore once on a staging website rather than waiting for a problem on the live blog.

  5. STEP 5Record the term and the renewal details

    Write down the date the 48 months term ends, because the plan is paid upfront for the whole 4-year term and the renewal date will be a long way off. Confirm with hosting.com what the plan will cost at renewal and whether it renews automatically, and keep that note with the blog's other records so the decision is not a surprise later.

Frequently asked questions

  • Does hosting.com Pro have to be paid all at once?

    Yes, according to the billing field. The plan is billed in USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, so the full 48 months is charged at signup. The monthly figure is a way of expressing the price, not a monthly payment schedule.

  • Can a solo blogger use the staging websites for a WordPress blog?

    The plan lists 20 staging websites. In general hosting terms, a staging site is a copy of a site used to test changes before they go live, which is useful for trying theme or plugin updates on a blog. The official data does not describe how staging is set up or managed on this plan, so confirm that with hosting.com.

  • Is there a shorter term than 48 months?

    The official data for hosting.com Pro and hosting.com Starter lists only a 48 months term. Among the alternatives on this page, the Namecheap Stellar, Stellar Plus and Stellar Business plans list a 12 months term, with billing described as USD per month equivalent and a renewal price that is higher than the first-term price.

  • Does the blogger need to manage a server on this plan?

    No. hosting.com Pro is Shared Hosting, and the provider manages the server. Day-to-day work happens inside the hosting account and the WordPress dashboard rather than through server administration.

  • How does 50 GB web space compare with the Namecheap plans?

    hosting.com Pro lists 50 GB web space. Namecheap Stellar Business lists 50 GB SSD, Namecheap Stellar lists 20 GB SSD and Namecheap Stellar Plus lists Unlimited SSD. Because hosting.com describes its storage as web space and Namecheap describes its storage as SSD, the figures are not directly comparable; read each as the provider states it.
