Who the hosting.com 8GB Cloud Server suits

This plan is aimed at someone who wants a server-sized resource allocation without taking on server administration. The feature list describes a fully managed server, which in plain terms means hosting.com handles the server side rather than the store owner. For a shop where the owner is busy packing orders and answering customer questions, that division of labor is the main reason to look at a managed plan instead of a self-managed one.

The plan also lists Unlimited websites, so a store owner who runs a main shop plus a separate landing page, a blog or a second brand can keep them on the same account. Emails and security included means mailboxes for order confirmations and customer replies are part of the package, and free domain registration means a new shop can start without buying a domain elsewhere.

It suits a store owner who is comfortable paying a full year upfront. The billing is per month on a 1-year term, paid upfront, so the whole first term is charged at purchase rather than spread across months. Someone who prefers a month-to-month arrangement or who is not sure the shop will still be running in a year should weigh that before signing up.

Resources listed on the pricing page

The official pricing page lists 4 dedicated CPU cores, 8 GB dedicated memory and 150 GB NVMe for this plan. The word dedicated appears in both the CPU and memory lines, which matters for a shop because checkout and cart pages are the moments when a store gets the most traffic and the least tolerance for slow responses. The data does not include a bandwidth or data transfer figure, so store owners who expect heavy image or video traffic should confirm that with the provider before paying.

Storage is stated as 150 GB NVMe. Product photos, order history and the daily backups all take space, so a store with a large catalog should compare its current usage with that figure. The data does not say whether backups count against the storage allowance, so that is another point to confirm.

The three checkout protection checks

HTTPS first. A checkout page served without a valid certificate shows browser warnings, and customers abandon carts when they see them. The feature list says emails and security included, but it does not spell out whether an SSL or TLS certificate is part of that wording. Before signing up, ask hosting.com directly whether a certificate is included and whether it renews automatically; after setup, confirm the live site answers over HTTPS by running this from any computer and replacing example.com with the shop's own domain: curl -I https://example.com

Backups second. The plan lists daily backups included, which covers the main risk of losing orders, customer records or a product catalog to a bad plugin update or a mistake. What the data does not describe is how a restore is requested, how far back the backups go or whether a store owner can restore a single file instead of the whole site. Those details decide how fast a shop can recover, so ask about them and keep an independent export of the store database as well.

Renewal cost third. The renewal price for this plan is higher than the first-term price. Because the first term is 12 months and paid upfront, the higher amount arrives in one go at the end of that term. Read the renewal price and term in the price table on this page and put the renewal date in the shop's calendar so the charge does not arrive as a surprise during a busy sales season.

How billing works

The official pricing page describes the charge as USD per month on a 1-year term, paid upfront. In practice that means the monthly price shown in the table is multiplied across the term and collected at the start, not drawn from a card each month. The term is 12 months.

At the end of the term the plan renews at a price that is higher than the introductory price. The exact figures are in the price table, which is kept verified separately from this text. The data does not state whether renewal is automatic or whether a reminder is sent, so confirm both with hosting.com and note the renewal date yourself.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Build and test the store before changing DNS

    Install the shop platform on the new server and load products, payment settings and shipping rules while the old site, if any, keeps serving customers. Test a full order from cart to confirmation using a temporary address or a preview method the provider offers. Only move the domain once a test order completes cleanly.

  2. STEP 2Connect the domain

    The plan lists free domain registration, so a new shop can register its domain with hosting.com during signup; an existing domain is pointed by updating its nameservers or DNS records at the current registrar. Before changing anything, record the current settings so they can be restored if needed, and check where the domain points by running this from any computer, replacing example.com with the shop's domain: dig +short A example.com. Wait until that command returns the new server's address before moving on.

  3. STEP 3Enable HTTPS once the domain resolves

    Issue or activate the certificate only after the domain points at the new server, since most certificate checks fail otherwise. Because the data does not confirm a certificate is included, this is the moment to use whatever hosting.com confirmed before purchase. Then set the shop to force HTTPS on every page, not just checkout, and test the cart and payment pages in a private browser window.

  4. STEP 4Understand backups and restores

    Daily backups included is on the feature list, but how to request or perform a restore is not described in the data. Ask support to walk through a restore before it is needed, and note where the backups are kept and how far back they reach. Separately export the store database on a schedule so an order record can be recovered even if the full-site backup is older than the last sale.

  5. STEP 5Record the term and renewal details

    The plan runs on a 12 months term, paid upfront, and renews at a price higher than the first-term price. Write the renewal date in the shop calendar and read the renewal price and term in the price table on this page so the charge is budgeted ahead of time. Confirm with hosting.com whether renewal is automatic and whether a reminder is sent.
