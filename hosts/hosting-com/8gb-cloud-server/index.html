What the official data lists for this plan

On the resource side, the pricing page lists 4 dedicated CPU cores, 8 GB dedicated memory and 150 GB NVMe. The word dedicated matters for a store: these resources are listed as assigned to this server rather than shared with other customers, which is the kind of detail that affects how a checkout behaves during a sale or a busy evening.

The feature list is short and the same across the whole hosting.com Cloud Server range: Unlimited websites, fully managed server, emails and security included, free domain registration, and daily backups included. Each of these is discussed below in the order a store owner tends to care about them.

The term is 12 months. Billing is described as USD per month on a 1-year term, paid upfront, and the plan renews at a rate that is higher than the first-term rate. The exact amounts are in the price table.

Who this plan suits

This plan fits a store owner who wants a provider-managed server rather than a shared hosting account, and who does not want to patch or maintain the server personally. The fully managed server feature is the main reason to look at this category instead of a cheaper shared plan or a self-managed VPS.

Unlimited websites means a main shop can sit alongside a brand site, a landing page for a campaign, or a second storefront without buying another plan. Emails and security included and free domain registration mean the basics of a store launch are listed as part of the package rather than as add-ons.

It is less suited to someone who wants to pay month to month and walk away after a short trial. The plan is paid upfront for the full term, so it fits a store that expects to be online for at least that long.

HTTPS on checkout pages

Checkout and account pages should only ever load over HTTPS, and most payment providers require it. The feature list says emails and security included, but it does not spell out whether an SSL or TLS certificate, automatic renewal of that certificate, or a forced redirect from the plain address are part of that bundle. Ask hosting.com to confirm this in writing before signing up, since a certificate that lapses silently is one of the fastest ways to lose a day of orders.

Once the store is live, test HTTPS from your own computer rather than trusting a green padlock in one browser. Replace example.com with the store's domain and run: curl -I https://example.com

The output should show a successful response over HTTPS. If it shows a redirect, follow it and check that the final address is the HTTPS version of the store, not the plain one. Repeat the test for the checkout path if it uses a different hostname.

Backups and restores

The plan lists daily backups included. For a store, a backup is only useful if orders and customer accounts can be restored to a known point after a bad plugin update or a mistaken bulk edit. The official data does not describe how a restore is requested, how many days of backups are kept, or whether a single file or database can be restored on its own, so settle those questions with hosting.com before relying on the included backups.

Whatever the answer, keep an independent export of the store database and product images somewhere outside the server. Provider backups protect against a server problem; your own copy protects against losing access to the account itself, for example at the end of a term.

Billing, term and renewal

Billing is USD per month on a 1-year term, paid upfront. In practice that means the whole 12 months is charged at signup, not spread across the year, so the amount leaving the business account on day one is the full first-term total shown in the price table.

The renewal price is higher than the first-term price. Read the renewal price and the term in the table before paying, and treat the renewal figure, not the first-term figure, as the ongoing hosting cost in the store's budget. The first term is effectively a discounted year.

Because the plan is paid upfront for 12 months, put the end of the term in a calendar with a reminder a few weeks ahead. That leaves time to decide whether to renew at the higher rate, move to a different hosting.com size, or migrate elsewhere while the current term is still active.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Register or prepare the store domain

    Use the free domain registration feature if the store needs a new domain, or keep the existing domain at its current registrar if the store is moving. Do not change nameservers or DNS records yet. Write down the server address hosting.com gives after signup; it is needed in a later step.

  2. STEP 2Build the store on the new server before moving traffic

    Install the store software, import products and configure payment and shipping while the old site, if there is one, stays live. Keep the first exported copy of the store database somewhere outside the server. Only move on when test orders complete correctly on the new server.

  3. STEP 3Point the domain and enable HTTPS

    Point the domain's nameservers or its A/AAAA records at the hosting.com server, then confirm the change from your own computer by replacing example.com with the store's domain and running: dig +short A example.com. Once the domain resolves to the new server, enable the SSL certificate and force every page, especially checkout, onto HTTPS. Test the result the same way, replacing example.com with the store's domain: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Confirm how backups and restores work

    Locate the daily backups included with the plan and ask hosting.com how a restore is triggered and how far back backups reach. Run a test restore of a single product or order before the store is busy, so the process is familiar when it is needed under pressure. Keep scheduling your own database exports alongside the provider's backups.

  5. STEP 5Record the term end and renewal price

    The plan is paid upfront for 12 months, so note the date the term ends in the business calendar with a reminder well ahead of it. Copy the renewal price from the price table into the store's budget, since it is higher than the first-term price. Decide before the reminder fires whether to renew, change size, or move.
