What the official data lists for hosting.com Starter

hosting.com Starter sits in the Shared Hosting category at hosting.com. The feature list is short: 5 websites and 3 staging websites. Storage is listed as 15 GB web space, which is the total web space for the account rather than an allowance per site. The term is 48 months, and billing is described as USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, so the monthly figure in the price table is a way of expressing a cost that is actually paid in one go at the start.

Several things an agency normally wants to know are simply absent from the listing. There is no bandwidth figure, no CPU or RAM allocation, and no mention of email accounts or a named control panel. That does not mean those things are missing from the plan; it means this page cannot confirm them, so ask hosting.com directly before signing a client up and keep the answers in the client's record.

Who hosting.com Starter suits

The plan fits an agency that runs a handful of modest client sites and prefers one bill for all of them. 5 websites covers, for example, a few brochure sites and a small blog or two for local businesses, and 3 staging websites give the team somewhere to show a client a redesign or test a plugin update before it goes live. 15 GB web space is the number to measure against the combined size of those sites, including uploads, themes and any exports left on the server.

It is a weaker fit where the client roster is likely to grow past 5 websites during the term, where clients expect heavy media libraries that would crowd the 15 GB web space, or where each client is invoiced on a cycle very different from the agency's own 48 months commitment. An agency in that position should read the comparison section below before deciding.

Billing, term and renewal: what to check before paying

Because billing is USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, the agency pays for the full 48 months at the start. For cash-flow planning that matters: client retainers are usually monthly or yearly, so the agency carries the cost up front and recovers it over the term. Confirm with hosting.com whether any refund or cancellation window applies, since the listing says nothing about one.

The official data does not list a renewal price for hosting.com Starter. Ask hosting.com what the plan costs when the 48 months end, whether renewal is again a 4-year term paid upfront, and whether a shorter renewal cycle is offered. Get that answer in writing before paying, because the renewal falls far enough in the future that the person who signed up may no longer be handling the account.

Keeping access, backups and renewal dates organised across client sites

With 5 websites under one account, every client site shares a single hosting login. The listing does not say whether separate user roles, per-site FTP or SFTP accounts, or client-level access are available, so an agency that wants to hand a client limited access to their own site should ask before relying on it. In the meantime, keep the main login in the agency's password manager, record which staff members hold it, and note which of the 5 websites belongs to which client.

Backups are not confirmed in the listing, so treat any provider backup as a bonus rather than the plan. A workable routine is to export each client site's files and database on a schedule, store the copies away from the hosting account, and label them by client and date. The 3 staging websites are a sensible place to rehearse a restore: pull a client's backup into a staging website, confirm it loads, and the agency knows the copy is usable before an emergency.

Renewal tracking is simpler here than with a mix of providers, because all 5 websites share one 48 months term and one renewal date. Put that date in the agency's shared calendar with reminders well ahead of it, note the renewal price once hosting.com confirms it, and map each client contract against the date so no client site is left on an account that lapses after someone leaves the agency. If a client moves elsewhere mid-term, confirm with hosting.com whether that site slot can be reused for a new client.

Plan the 3 staging websites deliberately. An agency that wants a permanent staging copy for every one of its client sites at the same time should check with hosting.com how staging websites are counted, and otherwise assign staging to the sites in active development and clear a staging copy once its changes are live.

Troubleshooting scenarios for a multi-client account

A client's domain still shows the old host after migration. The usual cause is that the registrar still points at the previous nameservers or that the old DNS records were copied across without updating the A record. Check the registrar first, then the DNS zone, and allow for propagation before escalating to hosting.com support.

A client site needs more room than expected. Since all 5 websites draw on the same 15 GB web space, one client's media library can squeeze the others. Audit uploads, remove stale exports stored inside the web root, and if the account is close to the limit, weigh hosting.com Pro's 50 GB web space against the cost of moving that client to a separate account.

A staff member who held the login leaves. Rotate the hosting password immediately, update the password manager entry, and review any API keys or FTP credentials tied to the account. This is also the moment to re-check that the renewal date and the confirmed renewal price are recorded somewhere other than that person's inbox.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Build each client site on a staging website first

    Use one of the 3 staging websites to assemble the client's site, load content and run through forms and contact flows before anything is public. Keep a note of which staging website belongs to which client so the team does not overwrite another client's work. When the client signs off, set up the live site as one of the 5 websites and copy the approved version across.

  2. STEP 2Point the client's domain at hosting.com

    At the client's registrar, either switch the nameservers to the ones hosting.com provides or keep the existing DNS and update the A record to the address hosting.com gives for the account. Agencies often do not hold the client's registrar login, so request access or send the client exact instructions. To confirm which nameservers the domain currently uses, run this from your own computer, replacing example.com with the client's domain: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable HTTPS once DNS resolves

    The listing does not mention an SSL certificate, so confirm with hosting.com before signing up whether one is included and how it is issued; some hosts require the domain to resolve to their server first, which is why this step follows the DNS change. Once the certificate is active, check from your computer that the site answers over HTTPS and redirects correctly, replacing example.com with the client's domain: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Set up and test backups

    Ask hosting.com how backups and restores work on this plan, how far back they go and whether restores are per site or per account. Whatever the answer, schedule your own exports of each client's files and database to storage the agency controls. Test a restore on a staging website before it is needed for real.

  5. STEP 5Record the billing details in the client file

    Note that the plan is paid upfront for a 48 months term, store the invoice, and put the date the term ends in the shared calendar with reminders. Confirm the renewal price directly with hosting.com and record it next to that date, so that whoever handles the account later is not caught out. Link each client's own contract end date to this renewal so the two can be reviewed together.
