What the official data lists for hosting.com Starter

The plan is listed with three concrete resources: 5 websites, 3 staging websites and 15 GB web space. The website count is the number of separate sites the account can host, the staging count is the number of staging copies that can exist alongside them, and the web space figure is the storage shared by everything on the account.

Billing is described as USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront. In practice that means the per-month figure is a way of expressing the cost, but the whole 48-month term is paid in a single payment at checkout. The price table on this page shows the upfront amount as verified.

The official data does not list a bandwidth figure for this plan, so there is no transfer allowance or unmetered claim to report. Anyone expecting heavy traffic across several client sites should confirm with hosting.com how transfer is handled before signing up. The data also does not mention CPU or RAM figures for this shared plan.

Who hosting.com Starter suits

The plan fits an agency that wants a single account for a small, stable roster of client sites. With 5 websites allowed, it can hold up to five low-traffic client properties such as brochure sites, landing pages or small portfolios, provided their combined files stay inside 15 GB web space.

The 3 staging websites are the part of the feature list most relevant to agency work. They allow changes to be prepared and reviewed on a separate copy before going live, which is the usual way to show a client a redesign or a plugin update without touching the production site. Because there are fewer staging websites than websites, an agency would need to rotate staging between clients rather than keeping one permanently per site.

The 48-month term paid upfront suits an agency that prefers to settle hosting once and not revisit billing for a long stretch, for example when hosting is bundled into a multi-year client agreement. It is a poor fit for an agency that expects the client list to change often, needs more than 5 websites on one account, or wants to keep the option of leaving after a year.

What to check before signing up

Check the billing model first. The plan is charged in USD per month on a 4-year term and paid upfront, so the money for all 48 months leaves the agency account at once. If client contracts are shorter than the hosting term, decide in advance how that cost is allocated and what happens if a client leaves before the term ends.

The official data does not list a renewal price for hosting.com Starter, so there is no way to say from this page whether the price after the first term is higher, lower or the same. Confirm the renewal price and the renewal process directly with hosting.com before paying, and write the answer into the agency's billing records.

Confirm how hosting.com counts the 5 websites and the 3 staging websites, for example whether subdomains or parked domains count toward the limit, and whether a staging copy can be promoted to live. The data states the numbers but not the mechanics, so the answers should come from the provider.

Finally, treat 15 GB web space as the total for the entire account rather than per site. Media-heavy client sites can use a large share of that figure on their own, so add up the current size of each site that would move to the account and compare it with the listed storage before committing.

Keeping backups, renewal dates and access organised across client sites

Backups are the first thing to settle. The official data does not mention backups or restores for this plan, so do not assume the host keeps copies of client sites. Before signing up, ask hosting.com what backup and restore options exist, how often they run and how a single site can be restored without touching the other four. Whatever the answer, keep an agency-owned off-host copy of each client site's files and database, labelled by client and date, so a restore never depends on one provider.

Renewal dates are simpler on this plan than on a mix of monthly accounts, because there is a single term of 48 months covering every site on the account. The flip side is that one missed renewal affects all five client sites at once. Record the end date of the term in the agency calendar with reminders well ahead of it, note which clients are on the account, and attach the renewal price confirmed with hosting.com so the person handling billing later has the figures in one place.

Access control is where the official data is silent. It does not confirm whether the plan supports separate users, team logins or per-client permissions. If more than one person at the agency will manage the account, or if clients expect their own login, check this with hosting.com before signing up. Until then, plan on a single primary login stored in the agency password manager, with a written map of which client site lives under which domain and which staging copy belongs to which client.

Quick checks an agency can run from its own machine

When a client reports that their site is not loading after a move, the first question is where the domain currently points. From any laptop, replace example.com with the client's domain to see which nameservers it uses right now: dig +short NS example.com

If the nameservers are correct but the client sees a security warning, check what the server returns over HTTPS and whether it redirects, again replacing example.com with the client's domain: curl -I https://example.com

Running these two checks before contacting support saves time, because they separate a DNS problem at the registrar from a certificate or redirect problem on the hosting account. Keep the output in the client's ticket so the next person on the team can see what was already verified.
