What the official data lists for hosting.com Starter

The plan is in the Shared Hosting category, which means hosting.com manages the server and the account holder works with the sites rather than the operating system. The listed resources are 15 GB web space, with 5 websites and 3 staging websites as the stated allowances. Billing is USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, and the term is given as 48 months.

Several details an agency usually relies on are not part of the official data: there is no entry for cpu, ram or bandwidth, and nothing describes the control panel, backup tooling, SSL certificates, email, client sub-accounts or server locations. Those gaps are not a judgement on the plan; they simply mean each item has to be confirmed with hosting.com before client sites are moved. The remaining sections point out where each gap matters most.

Who hosting.com Starter suits

The 5 websites allowance fits an agency with a small, stable roster of client sites that share one account, for example a handful of brochure sites or small business sites that do not need large storage. The 15 GB web space has to cover every site on the account, including media libraries and any files kept in the staging copies, so a roster of image-heavy sites may fill it sooner than a roster of text-led sites.

The 3 staging websites allowance is the feature that most distinguishes this plan from the Namecheap plans in the same category, which list no staging feature in the data. An agency that tests theme or plugin changes before pushing them to a client site can map those staging slots to its most actively developed projects. The data does not describe how staging copies are created or synced, so the workflow itself is something to confirm.

An agency planning to add clients steadily should note the hard limits before signing up. Once 5 websites are in use, the next client either needs a second account, a move to hosting.com Pro, or another provider. Because the plan is paid upfront for the full term, a mid-term move does not change what has already been paid, so matching the plan to the expected roster size is worth doing before paying.

Renewal dates: one upfront payment on a 48 months term

Billing on this plan is USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront. In practice the per-month figure in the price table is the basis of a single upfront payment that covers 48 months, after which the account comes up for renewal. For an agency this is a useful simplification: there is one renewal date for the whole account instead of a separate date for each client site, and that date sits years away rather than months.

The official data does not list a renewal price for hosting.com Starter, so confirm it with hosting.com before paying. The long term makes this more important, not less, because the renewal decision will land on a date far from the day the account was opened, possibly after staff or client contracts have changed.

A practical habit is to record the purchase date, the term end date and the plan name in whichever system the agency uses for domain renewals, and to add reminders well before the term ends so client contracts can be adjusted. Domains are usually registered separately from hosting, so their renewal dates should be tracked alongside the hosting date rather than assumed to match it.

Backups and access: what to confirm before moving client sites

The features list for hosting.com Starter mentions only 5 websites and 3 staging websites. It does not confirm included backups, restore options or how often any backup would run, so an agency should ask hosting.com directly what is included and plan its own offsite copies regardless. A client who loses a site expects the agency to have a copy that does not depend on the host, so a schedule for exporting files and databases for each of the sites on the account is sensible on any Shared Hosting plan.

Access is the second item to settle. The data does not say whether the control panel supports separate logins or client-level access, which matters when several people at the agency and occasionally a client need to reach one site but not the others. Until hosting.com confirms the options, treat the account as a single login, keep its credentials in a shared password manager with a named owner, and keep a per-site record of which client owns which domain, which staging slot is attached to it, and where the latest offsite backup lives.

It offers fewer websites than hosting.com Starter but more storage, and the data marks its renewal price as higher than the first-term price. It fits an agency with a very small roster that prefers a shorter term and a yearly chance to reconsider.

Namecheap Stellar Business lists Unlimited websites, 50 GB SSD and Unmetered bandwidth on 12 months, also with a higher renewal price. Both suit an agency whose roster is growing faster than a fixed websites allowance can follow, with the trade-off that the renewal price should be confirmed with Namecheap and the shorter term means renewal dates arrive every year.

hosting.com Pro is the step up within the same provider: Up to 10 websites, 20 staging websites and 50 GB web space on the same 48 months term, billed USD per month on a 4-year term and paid upfront. It fits an agency that likes the upfront single-payment model but expects to pass the 5 websites or 15 GB web space limits of Starter during the term. Like Starter, it has no renewal price in the data. Neither hosting.com plan lists bandwidth, whereas all three Namecheap plans list Unmetered bandwidth, which is a point to check for client sites with heavy traffic.

Checklist before signing up

Count the client sites that will live on the account and the ones expected to arrive during the next 48 months, then compare that number with the 5 websites allowance. Estimate total storage across those sites, including media and staging copies, against 15 GB web space. Decide which projects get the 3 staging websites slots.

Confirm with hosting.com what the upfront payment covers, what the renewal price will be, and whether backups, SSL certificates, email and multi-user access are included, since none of those appear in the official data. Read the term and the current per-month figure in the price table, record the term end date in the agency calendar, and only then open the account and start moving sites.

Practical setup guide

  1. STEP 1Add the client site and assign a staging slot

    After the account is active, add the first client website to the account and decide whether it should use one of the 3 staging websites slots. Build or migrate the site fully before changing any DNS, so the client's current site keeps serving visitors in the meantime. Record the site, the client, the login owner and the assigned staging slot in the agency's site register.

  2. STEP 2Point the client domain at the new account

    Once the site is ready, update the domain's nameservers or DNS records at the registrar to the values hosting.com supplies for the account. Agencies often do not own the client's domain, so arrange registrar access or send the client exact instructions ahead of the switch. To confirm which nameservers a domain currently uses, replace the placeholder with the client's domain and run: dig +short NS example.com

  3. STEP 3Enable HTTPS after DNS points at the site

    The official data does not confirm an included SSL certificate, so check with hosting.com how certificates are provided before launch. When the domain resolves to the new account, enable or install the certificate for the site and make sure the site redirects to HTTPS. To check the response headers and any redirect, replace the placeholder with the client's domain and run: curl -I https://example.com

  4. STEP 4Set up backups and a restore test

    Ask hosting.com how backups and restores work on the account, then schedule the agency's own offsite exports of files and databases for every site that goes live. Perform a restore of one site into a staging slot once, so the process is known before a client emergency. Store the backup location for each site in the same register used for access details.

  5. STEP 5Record the term and renewal details

    The account is paid upfront for 48 months, so note the purchase date and the term end date in the agency's renewal calendar with reminders well in advance. Confirm the renewal price with hosting.com, since the official data does not include it, and record what the provider says alongside the date. Keep client domain renewal dates in the same calendar, as they are managed separately from hosting.

    The official data lists 5 websites and 3 staging websites. Those are the stated allowances, so an agency with more client sites than that would need a second account or the hosting.com Pro plan, which lists Up to 10 websites and 20 staging websites.

  • How is hosting.com Starter billed and when does it renew?

    Billing is USD per month on a 4-year term, paid upfront, and the term is 48 months. The account is paid once for the full term and renews when that term ends, which gives an agency a single hosting renewal date to track for the whole account.

Their renewal price is marked as higher than the first-term price, so the yearly cost after the first term should be confirmed with Namecheap. hosting.com Starter suits a fixed roster within 5 websites that wants staging and one upfront payment.
